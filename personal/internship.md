## 🌍 Visa Considerations for Russian Citizens (2026)

| Country | Visa Type | Difficulty | Notes |
|---------|-----------|------------|-------|
| **South Korea** | C-3 (short-term) or D-2 (student) | ⭐⭐ Easy | K-ETA for 90 days; research visa through sponsoring uni |
| **Japan** | Research visa | ⭐⭐ Easy | University sponsorship required; relatively straightforward for students |
| **Singapore** | Student Pass | ⭐⭐ Easy | University applies on your behalf; usually approved |
| **China** | X1/X2 (student) | ⭐⭐⭐ Medium | University invitation letter needed; processing ~4–6 weeks |
| **Australia** | Student Visa (subclass 500) | ⭐⭐⭐ Medium | Requires Confirmation of Enrolment (CoE) from host |
| **Indonesia** | VITAS (limited stay) | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Hard | Not typical for short research visits; consider other options |
| **Shengen (EU)** | C-type (short stay) | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Hard | Long processing times; student/research visas possible but slow |
| **UK** | Standard Visitor / Student | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Hard | No direct flights; student visas possible but expensive |
| **USA** | J-1 (Exchange Visitor) | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Very Hard | B1/B2 nearly impossible for Russians; J-1 through university only viable path |
| **South America** | varies by country | ⭐⭐⭐ Medium | Brazil, Argentina relatively open; research visas possible |

**Key insight:** For Russian citizens, **Asia (Korea, Japan, Singapore, China)** offers the most accessible visa paths for research internships. Universities typically sponsor the visa, making the process much easier than individual applications.
